## 12/11 — A believable dining room with seating preferences

**Objective.** Replace the grid of identical tables with a floor plan a customer would recognise,
and expose *where* a table is as a first-class criterion.

**Prompt.**

> Rework the restaurant floor as a believable dining room instead of a uniform grid. Constraints:
> a room of 22 × 14 m; **16 tables** with stable identifiers `T01`…`T16` (never derived from
> coordinates), each with position, rotation, capacity (2, 4 or 6), shape (`round` or `rect`),
> a zone and a list of features. Use five zones: `window` along the glazed wall, `corner`,
> `banquette` along the back wall, `centre` and `bar`. Features are cumulative: `quiet`,
> `private`, `view`, `near_wc`, `near_entrance`. Enforce at least 1.6 m between table centres,
> 1.2 m from any wall, and keep a 1.2 m circulation aisle between zones.
>
> Then build a recommendation engine as a **pure module** — no DOM, no Three.js — exposing a
> deterministic score for a table given a party size and a set of preferences: exclude
> unavailable tables and tables too small for the party, penalise oversized tables, reward a
> matching zone, reward each requested feature, penalise proximity to the toilets when the
> customer asks to avoid them. Tie-breaking must be total and stable (score, then capacity
> distance, then id). Return a list of human-readable reasons in French, never raw keys.
>
> Add a preferences panel: party size selector, toggle chips for zones and ambience, an explicit
> empty state, keyboard navigation, real checkboxes with labels. On every change, recompute the
> shortlist and highlight the matching tables in the 3D scene with a soft pulse that respects the
> table's status colour and never mutates it.

**Output.** `world/layout.js`, `interaction/recommend.js`, `interaction/highlight.js`,
`ui/preferences.js`, `scripts/check-layout.mjs`, enriched status panels.

**Verification.** 26 assertions in a real browser (unique ids, zone/capacity/features present,
deterministic ranking, window zone ranked first, French-only reasons); `check:layout` proving
distances; 0 ESLint errors.

## 17/11 — Barcelona reference and a camera with a body

**Objective.** Ground the décor in a real place instead of inventing it, and stop the camera from
feeling like a drone.

**Prompt.**

> I want the space to read as a specific restaurant rather than a generic bistro. Use **El Nacional
> in Barcelona** as the reference. Research it properly and give me sourced facts, not an
> impression: the building's history, its height, its structure, the number of vaults, the dining
> spaces, the materials of floors and walls, the counters, the lighting fixtures and their colour
> temperature, the dominant palette, and the signature service elements. Then give me the signature
> menu with real dish names and price ranges in euros. Finish with the ten elements to model first,
> ranked by visual impact, each with approximate dimensions and materials — and tell me explicitly
> what is *not* documented so I don't invent it.
>
> In parallel, fix the camera. Walking currently glides with no weight. I want the sensation of a
> head carried by a body: a subtle vertical bob indexed on **distance travelled** (so it stops when
> blocked against a wall), slow breathing when standing still, a small roll when turning, a slight
> pitch lean under acceleration, and smoothed mouse look. Clamp every offset to a few centimetres
> and a couple of degrees, never let the offsets affect collision, respect
> `prefers-reduced-motion`, and give me a key to disable the effect.

**Output.** Sourced research dossier (vaults, materials, palette, menu, priorities);
`interaction/view-motion.js`, `move-keys.js`, updated `first-person.js`, `docs/CONTROLS.md`.

**Verification.** Motion measured outside the browser: bob ±3.18 cm, 7 mm at rest, offsets exactly
zero when disabled, roll and lean clamped; 29 browser assertions.

## 17/11 — Rework the second level

**Objective.** Remove a piece of architecture that read as a mistake.

**Prompt.**

> The upper level does not work. It reads as a flat slab dropped into a box: a bare platform with
> a few posts, no fascia, no stairwell, no ceiling underneath. Either make it read as **built
> architecture** or remove it — but decide from the reference, not from taste.
>
> Before changing anything, re-read what the reference building actually is and tell me whether a
> mezzanine exists there at all. If it does not, delete the mezzanine and the staircase and rebuild
> the volume it implies: a single tall nave, the correct number of shallow vault bays, cast-iron
> columns along the walls, zenithal glazing, and a dark entrance vestibule that contrasts with the
> bright nave. Update the floor plan accordingly, keep the table identifiers stable, and update the
> walkable surfaces so the walk mode still has valid ground and colliders. Report the new height,
> the number of bays, and the table count.

6 iron columns, 5 skylights, dark vestibule; plan reduced to 20 tables; `walkable.js` reduced to a
single interior level; `docs/WORLD.md` rewritten with sources.

**Verification.** `check:layout` (20 tables, 8 m height, new surface probes), 29 browser

## 17/11 — WebGPU only, installable, augmented

**Objective.** Close the remaining engineering remarks: modern renderer without a silent
fallback, installable application, and an immersive mode that is not VR.

**Prompt.**

> Three changes, in this order.
>
> **1. WebGPU only.** Remove the WebGL fallback entirely: no `setupWebGLXRFallback`, no
> `forceWebGL`. The renderer must instantiate `WebGPURenderer`, verify the backend **after**
> `init()`, and distinguish three failure causes: `navigator.gpu` missing, no adapter returned,
> unexpected backend. On failure, remove the canvas, never schedule a frame, and show a full-frame
> screen titled “WebGPU required” that explains the cause and the exact procedure to enable it,
> with a retry button. The badge must only ever show WebGPU states and must display the real
> backend, never an intention.
>
> **2. Installable and offline.** Add a manifest with name, short name, relative `start_url` and
> `scope`, `display: standalone`, theme colours and three icons (192, 512, 512 maskable). Add a
> service worker that pre-caches the shell and the pinned Three.js modules, serves **HTML and
> modules network-first** (a stale module makes the team think the code did not change), keeps
> images on stale-while-revalidate and pinned CDN files on cache-first, and purges old caches on
> activation. Add an offline page, a registration module that does nothing outside a secure
> context, and a discreet “new version available” banner.
>
> **3. Augmented reality.** Add an `immersive-ar` mode: hit-test to detect a surface, a reticle
> following it, tap to place the restaurant as a scale model (0.12), transparent background, the
> environment shell and the 3D status panel hidden during the session, and a full restore on exit.
> Any failure — hit-test refused, session rejected — must produce a readable message and a clean
> session close, never a black screen.

**Output.** WebGPU-only `core/renderer.js` with `init()` verdict, `ui/webgpu-gate.js`,
`manifest.webmanifest`, `sw.js`, `offline.html`, `core/pwa.js`, `xr/ar.js` + `ar-hint.js` +
`ar-reticle.js`, `docs/PWA.md`, `docs/AR.md`.

**Verification.** 47/47 headers, 29 assertions, manifest and service worker served with 200,
no gate shown on a machine exposing an adapter.

## 17/11 — Decoration, furniture, façade, walk-and-click

**Objective.** Give the place a street, a real door, modern furniture, and make the walk mode
usable to pick a table.

**Prompt.**

> Finish the place so it looks lived-in rather than modelled.
>
> **Furniture.** The tables are dated: thick tops, tablecloths, boxy chairs. Rebuild them as
> contemporary bistro furniture: a thin terrazzo top (4 cm) with a brass edge — a torus ring on the
> round ones, brass strips on the rectangular ones — a tapered steel pedestal on a discreet base,
> and rectangular tables on flat legs with a floor stretcher. Chairs become thin shells with a
> padded seat, a backrest tilted a few degrees, and slim steel legs. Keep the state marker and the
> `userData` contract untouched: the rest of the code depends on them.
>
> **Exterior.** The building has no outside. Build a street façade: stone plinth, horizontal
> string courses, two framed shop windows with an iron grille, a red awning with a scalloped
> valance, an enamelled `EL NACIONAL` sign, two wall lanterns on brackets, two planters and a
> stone threshold with a mat. Add a proper exit door in the opening: brass frame, two glazed
> leaves with rails and stiles, vertical pull handles, a door closer, and a `SORTIE` sign readable
> from inside.
>
> **Decoration.** Add six brass wall sconces, three iron cage lanterns above the bar islands, four
> potted plants, two chalkboard menus and a wall clock above the mosaic.
>
> **Walk mode.** Start the visitor **outside**, on the terrace facing the door, so arrival is
> progressive. And fix table selection while walking: under pointer lock the cursor position is
> frozen, so the ray always comes from the same point. Cast from the centre of the screen instead,
> show a crosshair only in walk mode, and keep the click → card → explicit reservation flow.

**Output.** Rewritten `world/furniture.js` (+ `seat-layout.js`), `world/vestibule.js`,
`world/facade.js`, `world/decor.js` (+ `decor-details.js`), centre-cast selection in
`interaction/pointer.js`, crosshair owned by `ui/view-toggle.js`, walk start moved outside.

**Verification.** 47/47 headers, `Plan conforme`, 29 assertions, centred ray confirmed in
`pointer.js`, start position confirmed in `main.js`.

## 17/11 — Leave the rectangle

**Objective.** Break the parallel-epiped plan.

**Prompt.**

> The room is still a box: four straight walls and a rectangular floor. The vaulted ceiling is
> curved, but the footprint is not, and that is what the eye registers first.
>
> Break the geometry without touching the load-bearing walls, the floor plan or the collision
> system: round the four interior corners with a 2.2 m radius, approximated in segments with a
> stone plinth following the curve; add two semicircular arcades of eleven voussoirs above the
> back mosaic; replace the straight door lintel with an arched opening built from jambs and
> voussoirs with a brass bead. Add collision volumes so the walk mode cannot pass through the
> rounded corners. Keep every file under the 200-line limit — extract a dedicated module if
> needed.

**Output.** `world/arcature.js` (rounded corners, arch builder, arched opening),
`architecture.js` wired to it, four corner colliders in `walkable.js`.

**Verification.** 46/46 headers at the time, `Plan conforme`, 29 assertions.
